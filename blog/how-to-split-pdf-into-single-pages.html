How to Split a PDF into Single Pages (Free, Fast & Private)

Published September 19, 2026 · 7 min read · By Abdullah Salim

Multi-page PDF files are great for archiving, but they quickly become a headache when you only need to send a single receipt, share one chapter of a manuscript, or file specific court exhibits. Sending an entire 80-page document when a client only requested pages 12 through 14 looks sloppy, violates privacy protocols, and often triggers email attachment size rejections.

Whether you need to break an entire document into individual one-page files or extract a handful of non-consecutive pages, this tutorial provides the quickest, completely free methods available—with zero risk of data leaks.

When Should You Split a PDF?

Dividing a master PDF file into distinct components is one of the most common document workflows:

  • Invoice and Receipt Batching: Accounting departments frequently receive monthly billing batches containing hundreds of invoices in one continuous document. Splitting them into individual files allows them to be indexed by invoice number.
  • Confidentiality and Redaction: When sharing legal discovery, medical records, or employee evaluations, you often cannot share the entire case folder. Splitting allows you to deliver only the authorized pages.
  • Overcoming Email Attachment Limits: If a 40 MB presentation is blocked by recipient email servers, splitting it into two 20 MB parts or extracting only the core summary slides bypasses email limits without degrading graphic quality.
  • Course Material Distribution: Teachers and professors can extract specific assigned chapters or worksheets from a heavy textbook PDF.

Method 2: Using the "Print to PDF" Browser Trick (Zero Tools)

If you only need to extract a single contiguous range from a PDF and have no internet connection, any desktop web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox) has a built-in virtual printer that can extract pages:

Step 1: Open the PDF in Your Web Browser

Drag the PDF into an empty tab in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Safari.

Step 2: Open the Print Dialog

Press Ctrl + P on Windows or Command + P on Mac to summon the print menu.

Step 3: Change Destination to "Save as PDF"

Under the printer destination dropdown, select Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF).

Step 4: Specify Custom Pages and Save

Under the Pages setting, switch from All to Custom. Enter the pages you want (e.g., 4-7). Click Save and choose your destination folder.

Why "Print to PDF" Falls Short

While great for extracting a single range, this method has notable flaws:

  • Cannot split into individual pages: If you have a 40-page PDF, you would have to repeat the print dialog 40 separate times!
  • Strips Interactive Elements: Printing often flattens interactive form fields, discards bookmarks/table-of-contents trees, and can degrade embedded color spaces.
  • Clunky on Mobile: Setting custom page ranges in mobile print preview sheets is awkward and error-prone.

Method 3: Splitting Pages on Mac with Apple Preview

Mac users can use Apple's built-in Preview application for manual drag-and-drop extraction:

  1. Open your PDF in Preview.
  2. Click View > Thumbnails to reveal the page sidebar on the left.
  3. Select the thumbnails you want to extract. Hold Command to select multiple individual pages, or hold Shift to select a range.
  4. Drag the highlighted thumbnail cards directly out of the Preview sidebar onto your desktop or into a Finder folder. macOS will automatically create a new PDF named after the extracted pages.
