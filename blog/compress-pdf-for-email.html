How to Compress a PDF for Email (Under 2 MB) — Free & Private

Published September 17, 2026 · 6 min read

A 9 MB invoice should not be a problem. But between inbox attachment caps and corporate mail servers, "the file is too big" is one of the most common reasons a document never reaches its recipient. The fix is a quick compression pass — and you don't need to upload anything to do it.

This guide shows the three-step method, a cheat sheet of the attachment limits you'll actually hit, and four fallback tricks for when compression alone still isn't enough.

Quick Answer:

Open PDFZaap's Compress PDF tool, set the quality slider to about 0.5, and download the smaller file. Everything runs inside your browser — the PDF is never uploaded to a server, and no account is needed.

Email attachment limits cheat sheet

Before you compress, know the wall you're trying to fit under. (Limits are the common defaults; your specific system may differ.)

Service / context Typical limit
Gmail 25 MB total per message
Outlook / Hotmail 20 MB
Corporate mail (Exchange / Office 365) 10 MB (many IT departments cap it lower, sometimes 2 MB)
Government & public portals 2 MB (frequently enforced at upload)

Practical rule of thumb: when a form or portal says "2 MB", compress to roughly 1.5 MB so you stay safely under the cap after the message wrapper adds a little overhead.

3 steps to compress a PDF for email

Step 1: Open the Compress PDF tool

Go to the Compress PDF tool and choose your PDF. The file stays on your device — you are not uploading it anywhere.

Step 2: Set quality to about 0.5

The quality slider runs from 0.1 to 1.0. Around 0.5 is the sweet spot for email: images are re-encoded aggressively enough to matter, while text stays crisp and legible. Drop toward 0.3 only if you need to hit a very tight limit.

Step 3: Download and check the size

Click compress, save the result, and confirm the file size before attaching. If it fits, you're done — attach and send.

Still too big? 4 tricks that work

Compression alone won't shrink a 100-page scanned folder to 2 MB. When it doesn't fit, combine these instead:

1. Delete pages you don't need

Use the Delete PDF Pages tool to drop blank pages, cover sheets, or duplicate copies. Unsent pages are the cheapest kilobytes you'll ever remove.

2. Convert to grayscale

Color is expensive in image-based PDFs. The Grayscale PDF tool strips color information from text-heavy documents with little visible change — often a big size win for invoices and reports.

3. Split or extract only what's required

If the recipient only needs pages 12–18, don't send the whole file. Use Split PDF or Extract Pages to send just the relevant range.

4. Merge, then compress once

If your "PDF" is actually five small PDFs you're attaching separately, merge them first and compress the combined file once. Compressing the whole document in one pass usually beats compressing each part individually, and one attachment beats five.

FAQ

What are common email attachment size limits?

Gmail allows up to 25 MB per message, Outlook/Hotmail up to 20 MB, many corporate mail systems cap attachments at 10 MB (sometimes as low as 2 MB), and government or public portals frequently require files under 2 MB.

Does compressing a PDF reduce text quality?

Text is vector data — it isn't degraded by image compression and stays sharp at any quality setting. The slider mainly affects embedded images, so at 0.5 your document usually looks identical to the original while being far smaller.

Can I compress a PDF without uploading it to a server?

Yes. PDFZaap's compressor runs entirely in your browser: the file is read locally, re-encoded locally, and downloaded locally. Nothing is transmitted, which also keeps it usable on corporate networks that block third-party file upload sites.

Does compression work on scanned documents?

Yes — scanned PDFs are mostly images, which is exactly what compression targets, so the reduction is often substantial. If you also need selectable text, run the OCR PDF tool to add a text layer.
