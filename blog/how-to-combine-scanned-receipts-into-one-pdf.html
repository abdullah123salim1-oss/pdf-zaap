Preparation: 4 Golden Rules for Capturing Receipts

Before you merge your receipts, spending 30 seconds capturing clean images saves substantial headaches during tax audits:

  • Flatten Against High Contrast Backgrounds: Place white paper thermal receipts on a dark wooden desk or dark mousepad. This allows camera edge-detection algorithms to crop straight borders.
  • Ensure Even Overhead Lighting: Avoid pointing your phone directly under harsh overhead lights that cast phone hand shadows over key total amounts or date lines.
  • Capture Thermal Receipts Promptly: Thermal cash register receipts (from restaurants, gas stations, and parking meters) fade completely within 6 to 12 months. Scanning or photographing them immediately ensures you retain a permanent tax record.
  • Include the 4 Audit Essentials: Ensure every image legibly displays: (1) Vendor name, (2) Transaction date, (3) Itemized description of goods/services, and (4) Total amount paid including taxes.

Essential Step: Compressing the Final Receipt Document

High-resolution smartphone cameras take 12 to 48 megapixel pictures. When you combine 15 or 20 receipt photos into a single PDF, the resulting file can easily balloon to 40 MB to 70 MB. Most corporate email systems cap incoming attachments at 10 MB or 20 MB.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to combine both photo receipts and PDF receipts?

If you have paper receipts photographed on your phone and electronic invoices downloaded as PDFs, convert the photos into a PDF first using PDFZaap's JPG to PDF tool. Then use the Merge PDF tool to combine the converted receipt pages with your digital invoice PDFs into a single chronological file.

Why is my combined receipt PDF too large to email to my accounting team?

Phone cameras capture receipts at 12 to 48 megapixels, so combining 20 photos can produce a 60 MB file. Pass your combined document through PDFZaap's Compress PDF tool (or convert to Grayscale first) to shrink the file below 2 MB while keeping vendor names, dates, and currency totals razor sharp.

Do tax authorities (like IRS or HMRC) accept digital PDF receipt scans?

Yes. The IRS (under Rev. Proc. 97-22) and UK HMRC legally accept electronic receipt copies provided they are legible, show vendor details, date, transaction amount, and can be retrieved upon audit request.
